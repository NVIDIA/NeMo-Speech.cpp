Once upon a time, in a small village nestled between rolling hills and a quiet river, there lived an old clockmaker named Elias. Every morning he opened the wooden shutters of his shop, brewed a pot of strong black tea, and sat down at his workbench to repair the watches and clocks that the villagers brought to him. Some were family heirlooms passed down through generations, while others were simple kitchen timers that had stopped ticking after years of faithful service. Elias treated each of them with the same patience and care. He believed that every clock had a story to tell, and that listening closely to its gears was the only way to understand what had gone wrong. One rainy afternoon, a young girl named Clara stepped into the shop, holding a tiny silver pocket watch wrapped in a faded handkerchief. It had belonged to her grandmother, she explained, and it had not worked for as long as anyone could remember. Elias opened the case, adjusted his magnifying glass, and smiled. Inside, tucked behind the balance wheel, was a folded scrap of paper no larger than a fingernail. Carefully, he lifted it out with a pair of tweezers and handed it to Clara. Written in delicate handwriting were the words: time is the only gift we cannot give back, so spend it with the people you love. Clara read the note twice, then looked up at Elias with tears in her eyes. That evening, the little watch began to tick again, and it has not stopped since.
